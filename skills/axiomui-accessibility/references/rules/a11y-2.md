# Carry meaning beyond color

- ID: a11y-2
- Category: Accessibility & inclusivity
- Tags: Accessibility, Icons, Semantics
- URL: https://axiomui.nischal.fyi/rules/a11y-2

When color communicates an error, state or category, add a second cue someone can perceive without distinguishing the hues. For a detected form error, identify the field and describe the problem in text.

- Do: Red text + error icon + message
- Don't: Only a red border on the input

## Why it works

Changing a border from gray to red marks a difference in hue, but it tells someone little about how to correct a card number. A specific message conveys both the affected field and the reason for the rejection. An icon can make that message easier to find.

- [WCAG 2.2 · Use of color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)
- [WCAG 2.2 · Error identification](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-identification.html)

## What breaks

Two equally dark status dots can be indistinguishable in monochrome. Even someone who distinguishes both colors may still have to remember which one means pending.

## How to apply it

1. Use text, shape, pattern or another suitable cue for the meaning carried by color.
2. Identify form errors in text and associate the explanation with the control.
3. Inspect without color and check labels with assistive technology.

## Review questions

1. Can someone tell the two states apart without their hues?
2. Does the message explain what needs correcting?
3. Does the nonvisual presentation carry the same meaning?
