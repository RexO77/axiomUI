# Let people pause what moves or updates on its own

- ID: a11y-6
- Category: Accessibility & inclusivity
- Tags: Accessibility, WCAG, Motion
- URL: https://axiomui.nischal.fyi/rules/a11y-6

For nonessential content presented alongside other content, offer a way to pause, stop or hide automatic movement, blinking or scrolling that lasts more than five seconds. For automatic updates, offer a control at any interval. Adjusting the update frequency also works.

- Do: Testimonial strip with Pause
- Don't: Quote changes every 4s · no control

## Why it works

The testimonial strip replaces the quote while someone is reading the shared-calendar instructions just below it. Pause holds the current quote so the person can finish either passage at their own pace.

- [WCAG 2.2 · Pause, stop, hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
- [WAI-ARIA APG · Carousel pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/carousel/)

## What breaks

A reader can lose the end of a testimonial before finishing the sentence, and each replacement can draw attention away from the instructions.

## How to apply it

1. Search for autoplay, setInterval and polling callbacks in carousels, tickers and live feeds. Follow each start path and check which user control can stop the visible change.
2. Put a visible, keyboard-operable Pause control with the strip and show whether rotation is paused. Keep it paused until the reader resumes it.
3. WCAG 2.2.2 applies the five-second threshold to automatic movement, blinking or scrolling alongside other content. Automatic updates alongside other content have their own clause at any interval, including the option to control update frequency. Both clauses except essential activity.

## Review questions

1. Load the strip, wait for a quote to change, then use Tab and Space to pause it. After two four-second intervals, is the same quote still visible?
2. Does the strip stay paused when focus leaves the control?
3. Can someone tell that the strip is paused before resuming it?
